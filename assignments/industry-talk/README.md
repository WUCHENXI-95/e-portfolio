## Reflection

### What I Learned

These two talks gave me a much clearer picture of what data engineering actually looks like in Malaysian companies. Before attending, I mostly focused on coding and algorithms in class, but the speakers showed me that the job involves a lot more than that.

The Izeno speaker explained how SMEs build lightweight pipelines with limited budgets, which is very different from the enterprise-scale setups we study in tutorials. The TM speaker walked us through how they handle real-time data from millions of users — the biggest headaches are dirty data, concurrency, and storage costs, not the algorithm itself. Hearing both sides helped me understand that there are different career paths in data engineering depending on company size.

Both speakers also told us what employers actually look for in fresh graduates. It is not just coding skills. They want people who can turn business problems into data solutions, communicate well with product and marketing teams, and keep iterating when things change. They also stressed that having a GitHub portfolio and a polished LinkedIn profile makes a real difference when applying for jobs, which pushed me to take this e-portfolio more seriously.

### What Could Be Better

- **More hands-on time:** Both sessions were purely lecture-based. I wish they had included a 30-minute mini practice where we could try a simple data processing task together.
- **Longer Q&A:** The Q&A felt rushed. I wanted to ask about internship opportunities and specific tech choices but ran out of time.
- **Pre-session materials:** If they shared a short case document before the talk, I could have prepared better questions instead of thinking of them afterwards.

### What I Plan to Do

- Search for Izeno and TM's public technical blogs to learn more about their real projects beyond what was covered in the talks.
- Build small demo projects on GitHub that replicate the scenarios the speakers described, like a mini telecom data pipeline.
- After updating my LinkedIn profile, I want to connect with the two speakers to ask follow-up questions about internships and technical learning.

### Overall

These talks made me realise I had been too focused on the technical side and not enough on the business side. In real companies, understanding why you are building a pipeline matters just as much as knowing how to build it. Moving forward, I want to balance both — learn the tools properly while also understanding the business context behind every data project.
